## Introduction to Eos Augment3d

Augment3d is a suite of tools introduced in Eos 3.0, which adds a 3D
programming and visual environment to the Eos Family of consoles, as
well as ETCnomad for Mac and PC.
With Augment3d, you can control your rig directly on your console, or
tethered on a graphics-centric computer. It allows you to focus lights
with a click or a touch, preview your lighting looks in 3D space, and
control your rig from your phone using augmented reality.
This class takes you through getting Augment3d operational, introduces
you to 3D environments, and building and using your model and lighting
rig.

### AUGMENT3D PROGRAMMING ENVIRONMENT FEATURES:

    - Add fixtures to the 3D environment in Patch, or by importing
        from a Vectorworks 2019, 2020, or 2021 file.
    - Create architecture and scenery using stock objects from the
        Augment3d library or import models with support of over 50 file
        types.
    - Set up graphics to fit your application, with many settings to
        balance quality and performance. Use visual assistance tools to
        identify fixtures and beams and set the ambient lighting to your
        choice of level and color.
    - Speed up programming using tools like click-to-focus, preview
        mode, and blind edit – including effects.
    - Attach objects or fixtures to desk channels, allowing you to move
        them for scene changes, or adjusting lighting positions
        throughout a performance or venue-to-venue.
    - Use focus palettes to reverse-calculate fixture positions in the 3D
        environment, using a real lighting rig.
    - Use augmented reality on your phone or tablet to focus lights,
        view rig-specific overlays on the camera, and view channel
        numbers at the source and the incident of the beam.

## Display Quality Settings

     The quality of the Augment3d environment will depend on the computer
     being used. You will get different quality depending on whether you are
     using an Eos Family console or a tethered laptop. To balance quality of
     graphics with speedy performance, you can adjust the Display Quality
     Settings.
     In the bottom right of the Augment3d window, there is a dot that will
     change color based on the current quality of the graphic performance.
     The metric is displayed in Frames Per Second (FPS).

### PERFORMANCE POPUP

     Hover over the dot with your mouse to see the Performance popup:
           ● High: 25-30 FPS

           ● Medium: 20-24 FPS

           ● Low: 15-19 FPS

           ● Very Low: 10-14 FPS

           ● Poor: 0-9 FPS

### QUALITY & RENDERING

     Quality determines to what level of detail the software will render the
     Augment3d model. Rendering determines how fast the software will
     refresh the model.

     To adjust the quality settings, click on the {Options} menu and then
     {Quality}

     - Quality – Low and Medium are available on consoles.
           - In addition to Low and Medium, High and Ultra are available on
             ETCnomad and Tethered devices.
     - Rendering – this sets the maximum Frames Per Second the software
           will attempt to achieve.

### LIGHTING

Lighting determines how beams intersect with surfaces, and whether
they cast shadows.
- Optimized (Pass-Through) – beams will appear on all objects that
    the beam intersects, including those behind other surfaces. And will
    not cast shadows
- Approximated – beams will end at the first object they intersect,
    and not continue.
- Realistic – beams will appear on all objects that they intersect with
    and will cast shadows.

### BEST PRACTICE IDEAS

    - The default Quality settings are Medium & Approximated. You can adjust
      the settings to meet the performance level you want, based on the
      capabilities of the hardware you are running.
    - Eos Family Consoles only have access to Low and Medium Quality
      settings.

## About 3D Environments

### ABOUT ORIGIN POINTS

In a three-dimensional model space, we need to be able to define an
object’s location in reference to some other known place. To serve this
purpose, model spaces have an Origin Point, which is a fixed location in
space. The Origin is so called, because it is the origin point for the X, Y,
and Z axes. At the Origin Point, the values of all axes are zero.
To describe an object’s Position in space, we can define the offset from
the origin for each axis. If we define X at 5’-0”, Y at 2’-0”, and Z at 4’-
0”, the object goes to that position in space. Each of these offsets can
be negative as well, getting the object on the other side of the origin.
In a typical proscenium theater, your x-axis might describe your stage left
and stage right movement, your y-axis might describe your upstage and
downstage movement, and your z-axis might describe your height above
or below the floor,

### ABOUT OBJECT POSE

     Spatial position isn’t the only important information about an object, we
     also want to know its Rotation along each axis.
     That means there are a total of six pieces of information that define the
     position and orientation of an object in space. This collection of data is
     often referred to as the object’s Pose information.

     and Rotation values are referenced to. Many drafting programs allow

### EDIT MODE - GLOBAL & LOCAL ORIGIN

  In a 3D model space we need to be able to define an object’s location in
  reference to some other know place. To serve this purpose, model spaces
  have an origin point which is a fixed location in space. The origin is so called
  because it is the origin point for the X, Y, and Z axes.At the origin point, the
  values of all the axes are zero.

  aligned with the object, allowing it to be modified on the X, Y, and Z axes
  relative to the object, not the entire model space. Scale always uses the Local
  Origin.

### CHOOSING PAN/TILT OR XYZ FOCUS SPACE

You can store Cue and Focus Palette data in Pan/Tilt focus space or XYZ
focus space. When stored in Pan/Tilt space, the fixture will apply the
stored values to those parameters, regardless of where the fixture is
located in space. When stored in XYZ focus space, the fixture will point at
the stored location in space, regardless of where the fixture is located.

In a Live or Blind display, the used space will be manual/stored, while the
unused space will be grayed out, but they are always tied together.

## Appendix 4: Vectorworks Model Export

You can export your non-fixture model elements from Vectorworks and
import them into Eos as model objects.

### PREPARING VECTORWORKS FOR EXPORT

Vectorworks’ export engine relies on model visibility states. When you
export, only objects that are visible will be exported, and all graphical and
pose properties of that object will be retained (with certain limitations
provided by the file type).
A few things to consider before exporting model objects from
Vectorworks:

    - Complexity – a lot of models have extreme detail, because they
        were created for engineering or building purposes (for example,
        some have been known to include bolts with threading – a detail
        you likely do not need for lighting the object). Consider what
        level of detail will be useful in Augment3d and adjust your model
        accordingly. Remember that every additional surface/triangle adds
        processing overhead to the device hosting Augment3d.
    - Visibility & Movability – Augment3d has the ability to show & hide
        objects, as well as attach them to a channel for movement.
        Before exporting, consider what separate components you want
        to control, and export them individually. For example, exporting
        the architecture, separate from masking, separate from moving
        scenic elements may be more useful than one big model export
        including them all.
    - Location – many file formats support remembering an object’s
        location and orientation, relative to Vectorworks’ origin point.
        This can be useful when you are exporting and subsequently
        importing objects that need to maintain relative location. For
        example, if you want to import a theater’s architecture, and then
        import individual linesets in their proper location – using a file
        format that supports origin makes the process a lot easier.
